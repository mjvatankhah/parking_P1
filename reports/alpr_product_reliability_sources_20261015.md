# Production ALPR reliability: primary-source evidence

Date: 2026-10-15. Scope: source research to support the review of both local videos. This note does not claim that the existing models or pipeline meet a 99% target.

## Evidence and implications

1. Capture quality is plate-local. Axis documents that plate pixel width depends on scene width and sensor resolution, and shutter selection depends on vehicle speed and camera alignment. Its own License Plate Verifier specifies at least 130 pixels across a one-row plate. These are vendor-specific requirements, not a universal cutoff for our Iranian-plate models. **Design inference:** define and validate a supported capture envelope using actual plate width, blur, exposure, clipping, angle, and time visible; full-frame 1080p alone is insufficient. Sources: [Axis capture white paper](https://whitepapers.axis.com/en-us/license-plate-capture), [Axis Verifier manual](https://help.axis.com/en-US/axis-license-plate-verifier).

2. Model confidence is not automatically a probability that the whole plate is correct. Guo et al. empirically show poor calibration in modern neural networks and evaluate calibration methods, including temperature scaling. Their work is classification research, not proof of calibration of this OCR system. **Design inference:** measure exact-full-plate correctness at vehicle-event level and calibrate event acceptance against held-out labeled events; detector confidence and mean character confidence cannot establish 99% event accuracy. Source: [Guo et al., ICML 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html).

3. Calibration can deteriorate under dataset shift. Ovadia et al. benchmark shifted classification datasets and find standard post-hoc calibration can fall short. **Design inference:** separate development, calibration, and final evaluation by camera/session/date and vehicle passage. Reserve unseen cameras and difficult conditions as explicit tests. A threshold working on one video is not evidence that it transfers to a new camera. Source: [Ovadia et al., NeurIPS 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/8558cb408c1d76621371888657d2eb1d-Abstract.html).

4. Selective prediction explicitly trades coverage for error among accepted predictions. SelectiveNet studies joint prediction and rejection with a risk-coverage objective. **Design inference:** report both automatic acceptance coverage and exact accuracy among accepted events; retain uncertain and unread events rather than silently converting them to confident plates. No need to adopt SelectiveNet itself to use this evaluation principle. Source: [Geifman and El-Yaniv, ICML 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/geifman19a.html).

5. Statistical validation must match the claim. NIST describes exact binomial confidence limits, suitable when failures are rare, and fixed binary performance acceptance plans. **Our calculation:** for n independent, representative trials with all successes, the one-sided 95% exact lower success bound is 0.05^(1/n). At n=299 it is about 0.99003. Thus 299/299 can support a lower bound above 99% only for the specified stationary population and predeclared success criterion; it does not certify all environments, every subgroup, or a system tuned on those trials. Adjacent frames of one car are not 299 independent vehicle events. More testing is required if failures occur, and condition-specific claims require corresponding evidence. Sources: [NIST exact binomial](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbino.htm), [NIST binary performance acceptance planning](https://www.nist.gov/publications/confirming-performance-threshold-binary-experimental-response).

## Proposed product contract (design recommendation)

Define a passage as the unit of truth and require exactly one correct event with the exact plate, correct vehicle association, and bounded latency. Count missed passages, duplicate events, wrong associations, confident wrong plates, unresolved events, and latency breaches separately. Report overall exact event success over all in-scope passages as well as accepted-result precision and coverage; do not inflate success by excluding cases merely because the algorithm rejected them. Publish out-of-envelope frequency too.

Preserve original evidence and provenance. Treat raw and rectified crops as alternative observations of the same frame, never independent votes. Aggregate across distinct times with crop-quality and conflict signals; temporal spacing does not itself prove statistical independence. A retry cascade should have a fixed latency/compute budget and explicit unresolved outcomes. Calibrate and validate the final cascade, since selecting the highest confidence from many variants changes the acceptance behavior.

Use both existing videos as regression/development evidence, then freeze the algorithm and evaluate on new labeled camera/day/night sessions. Measure per-condition outcomes and confidence intervals. Camera onboarding should verify the capture envelope and replay an acceptance sample. Roll out in shadow mode, audit accepted and rejected examples, monitor drift in image quality and error categories, and version models/configuration/evidence so regressions can be reproduced.

These recommendations are engineering inferences informed by the sources above; the sources do not certify this implementation or prescribe a universal ALPR architecture.
